Financing walkthrough

Every rep should be able to quote a payment from memory on the top two plans and know what each plan costs us. Payment equals loan amount times the payment factor. That is the whole formula.

The dealer fee goes on the contract, not on us. We gross the price up so that after the lender takes their fee, we still net our price. The math is contract = our price / (1 minus the dealer fee). Buy the rate down and the customer signs for a bigger number. That is the tradeoff you are presenting.

Read that table left to right and you have the whole conversation. The 0% dealer fee plan is the cheapest contract the customer can sign, $14,500. Wells Fargo 0160 is a true 0% APR, and it puts $3,002 on the contract to get there. Neither one is wrong. The customer picks.

Say this out loud

Cash price and financed price are not the same number, and the customer hears that from you, not from the paperwork. "This is $14,500 if you write a check. If you want the 0% for five years, the contract is $17,502, because the lender charges us to buy that rate down and that cost is in the price."

Deferred interest is the one that bites. On a no interest if paid in full plan, if the customer does not clear the balance before the promo ends, interest gets charged back to day one at 15.49%. Say it every single time.

GoodLeap adds 0.50% if they pay by check instead of autopay. Wells Fargo long term plans need a $7,500 minimum.

HOA information on the engineering form

The form asks "Is there an HOA?" and answering Yes makes the HOA Information field required. That field is where installs go to die when it is left blank.

The engineering form fires after arrival on every HVAC estimate job type: condenser, coil, furnace, furnace and coil, condenser and coil, air handler, heat pump, package unit, full split system, full system cut in, single and multi zone mini split, duct replacement, relocation, AC add on.

What belongs in HOA Information. Management company name and phone, whether architectural approval is required before work, the submittal lead time in days, any equipment location or screening restriction, approved work hours, and whether a crane or roof access needs separate sign off.

Permits. Same conversation. Changeouts in our area need a mechanical permit, the job needs to pass Title 24 compliance, and duct or refrigerant work triggers field verification. If the HOA has a 30 day approval window and the permit has its own lead time, those run at the same time, not back to back. Say that to the customer up front so the install date you promise is the install date they get.

Pricebook review: the Why and the Terms

Every option a customer sees carries two blocks of copy. The Why is the reason this option exists for their house. The Terms are what we are actually promising. Both get read out loud today.

The Why. It is written for the homeowner, not for us. Wrong: "high efficiency variable speed inverter system." Right: "your upstairs bedrooms stop being ten degrees hotter than the living room." One sentence, their house, their problem.

The Terms. What is included in the price, every time: standard professional installation, removal and haul away of the old equipment, new thermostat, permit and city inspection, 10 year manufacturer parts warranty, 1 year labor warranty. If an option promises something different from that list, the option is wrong or the list is wrong. Find out which before a customer does.

Manual J, Manual D, and the electrical load

Sizing by what is already on the wall is how you end up with a system that short cycles, a duct system that cannot move the air, and a panel that will not carry the heat pump. California wants the calculation anyway. Do it once, up front, and the install goes clean.

Three calculations, three different questions:

  • Manual J. How much heating and cooling does this house actually need. Square footage, insulation, windows, orientation. Not "it was a 4 ton, so it is a 4 ton."
  • Manual S. Which piece of equipment matches that load at our design conditions. This is where the AHRI matchup from item 14 comes back.
  • Manual D. Can the duct system carry the CFM. Supply vent count and sizes and the return come off the engineering form, so fill that form out right and this is half done.

Electrical load. Before you sell a heat pump or anything all electric, the panel has to carry it. The engineering form already asks for panel amperage, a photo of the panel with the cover on and off, and the distance from panel to outdoor unit. Fill those in and pull the breaker sizing sheet. A service upgrade found on install day is a change order and an angry customer. Found on the estimate it is just a line item.
